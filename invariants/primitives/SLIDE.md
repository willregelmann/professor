# Slide

Something to look at while one point is made, and notes on what to say about it. What's shown might be a title, a few lines, a diagram, a piece of code or an equation. A slide prepared a week ago and a slide sketched ten seconds ago in answer to a [question](QUESTION.md) are the same kind of thing. What separates them is how they came to be, not what they are.

## Invariants

- **Notes, not a script.** A slide's notes say what to cover. The words are found fresh each time it's given, and no slide holds narration to be read out.
- What's shown and what's said are different things. The lecturer talks about what's on the slide and never reads it out.
- **A slide that can't be shown says so.** A diagram that fails to draw is visibly a failure, to the listeners and to the lecturer, who can draw it again. It's never a blank space.
- A slide shows where its claims come from when they come from somewhere: a file and line, a paper, a page. It never shows a source it wasn't drawn from.
- A slide remembers how it came to be: prepared ahead of time, or made up during a [lecture](LECTURE.md), and for which question.
- A slide belongs to exactly one [deck](DECK.md).
- A slide can be corrected. When it is, what it used to say isn't shown as current anywhere.
