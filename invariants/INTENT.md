# Professor

Live lectures given by AI agents: a lecture that listens.

All the way through a lecture, Professor keeps one question open: **did that land?**

Delivering the material is only half of teaching. A deck with a voiceover can't tell that someone got lost three slides ago. It can't answer "wait, why?", and it can't draw the picture that would have made the point clear. A lecturer can. They speak from notes, not a script. They take a question when it comes, go back a slide, sketch something new, and then pick up the thread. "Explain this codebase" and "teach me quantum field theory" are the same request: someone who knows the subject, talking it through out loud with something to look at, and changing course when the room does.

It's built on one idea: fallibility isn't a flaw, it's a side effect. A lecture that can't be wrong is a recording, and a recording can't improvise, simplify or try a second explanation when the first one missed. So Professor works the way human lecturers do. It prepares slides and notes ahead of time, says them fresh each time, and makes up a new slide when a question deserves one. Where being wrong matters, it does what lecturers do: it shows where a claim comes from, says when it isn't sure, and can always be asked "is that right?". A lecture leaves its deck behind, including the slides made up on the spot, so it can be given again, picked up later or improved. Any agent that can speak can lecture, whichever runtime it lives in.

If a lecture would have gone exactly the same with nobody listening, Professor has failed at the one thing it exists to do.
